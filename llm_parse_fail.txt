We will look at some real-life situations and decide if they form an AP. First up, the taxi fare. The fare is Rs 15 for the first kilometre and Rs 8 for each additional kilometre. Let's see. The first term is 15. For the second km, we add 8, making it 23. For the third, another 8, making it 31. The difference is constant, just like an auto-rickshaw meter in Bangalore which clicks up by the same amount after the initial fare. So yes, this is an AP! Now, what about the air in a cylinder when a vacuum removes one-fourth of the *remaining* air each time? This is a bit tricky, like trying to guess how many sweets are left in a box during Diwali after your cousins have visited! The amount taken is a fraction of what's left, not a fixed number. Let's say we start with volume V. First, we have 3V/4 left. Then, we remove 1/4 of *that*, leaving 9V/16. The difference between terms is not constant. So, this is not an AP. How about digging a well? It costs Rs 150 for the first metre and rises by Rs 50 for each subsequent metre. The costs would be 150, 200, 250, and so on. The common difference is a fixed Rs 50. So, this is definitely an AP. Finally, let's consider money deposited at compound interest. With compound interest, the interest is calculated on the new, larger amount each year.
