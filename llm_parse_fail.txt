(1) Hydra and Ascaris (2) Hydra and Oryctolagus (3) Ascaris and Pheretima (4) Ascaris and Oryctolagus. <pause duration='4'/> The correct answer is option 4. Sexual dimorphism means the male and female of a species look different. In both Ascaris, the roundworm, and Oryctolagus, the rabbit, males and females have distinct external features. Think of it like peacocks and peahens you might see during monsoon – the male is so vibrant and colourful, while the female is plainer. That's sexual dimorphism! Hydra and Pheretima, the earthworm, are hermaphrodites. Question 24. Binary fission is found in – (1) Amoeba (2) Paramoecium (3) Planaria (4) All of these. <pause duration='3'/> The correct option is 4, All of these. Binary fission is a common method of asexual reproduction for all these simple organisms: Amoeba, Paramoecium, and Planaria. Question 25. Eutherian mammals are – (1) Oviparous. (2) Viviparous (3) Ovoviviparous (4) Both (1) and (3). <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is option 2, Viviparous. Eutherian mammals are placental mammals, which means they give birth to live young ones, just like humans, cows, and dogs. Question 26. In some species parthenogenesis may alternate with sexual reproduction this process is called – (1) Complete parthenogenesis (2) Incomplete or cyclic parthenogenesis (3) Both the above (4) None of the above. <pause duration='3'/> Correct! It's option 2, Incomplete or cyclic parthenogenesis. This is when an organism can switch between reproducing asexually through parthenogenesis and reproducing sexually, depending on the conditions. Question 27. Haploid parthenogenesis among insects is shown by order (1) Hymenoptera (2) Homoptera (3) Coleoptera (4) All the above. <pause duration='3'/> The right answer is 1, Hymenoptera. This order includes ants, bees, and wasps, where unfertilized eggs develop into haploid males. Question 28. In man sperms move after ejaculation at a rate of nearly – (1) 2 to 4 mm/minute (2) 2 to 4 feet/minute (3) 2 to 4 inches/minute (4) 2 to 4 cm/minute. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is option 1. Sperms move at about 2 to 4 millimeters per minute. It might not sound fast, but for a tiny cell, it's quite a journey! It's not exactly the speed of a Mumbai local during rush hour, but it's determined and focused on its destination! Question 29. What helps in the penetration of egg by the sperm – (1) Fertilizin (2) Antifertilizin (3) Sperm lysin (4) Fertilization membrane. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 3, Sperm lysin. These are enzymes released from the acrosome of the sperm that dissolve the egg's outer layers, allowing the sperm to penetrate. Question 30. Fertilizins are the substances emitted from – (1) Mature egg (2) Sperms (3) Immature egg (4) Polar body. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 1, Mature egg. The mature egg releases fertilizin, which is a chemical that helps in attracting the sperm and ensuring sperm from the same species can bind to the egg. Question 31. One of the minute cell which separates from the animal egg during maturation is known as – (1) Primary spermatogonia (2) Secondary oogonia (3) Primary oogonia (4) Polar bodies. <pause duration='3'/> The answer is 4, Polar bodies. During oogenesis, the cell divisions are unequal, producing one large egg and smaller polar bodies which typically degenerate. Question 32. Fertilization occurs in human, rabbit and other placental mammals in – (1) Ovary (2) Uterus (3) Fallopian tubes (4) Vagina. <pause duration='3'/> Correct! The answer is 3, Fallopian tubes. This is the site where the sperm meets the egg and fertilization takes place in most placental mammals, including humans. Question 33. Development of an egg without fertilization is called – (1) Gametogenesis (2) Metagenesis (3) Oogenesis (4) Parthenogenesis. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 4, Parthenogenesis. This is a form of asexual reproduction where an embryo develops from an unfertilized egg. Question 34. What is true in the process of fertilization – (1) Only one sperm reaches the egg and enters it. (2) The entry of sperm activates the egg for completing meiosis. (3) Two haploid nuclei fuse and immediately divide to produce two nuclei which are again haploid. (4) Only the acrosome of the sperm enters the egg. <pause duration='3'/> The correct statement is option 2. The entry of the sperm triggers the secondary oocyte to complete its second meiotic division, forming the mature ovum and a second polar body. Question 35. On fertilization of egg nucleus with sperm nucleus – (1) First maturation is completed (2) Second maturation is completed (3) Embryo is formed (4) First polar body is formed. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 3, Embryo is formed. The fusion of the egg and sperm nuclei creates the diploid zygote, which is the first cell of the new embryo. Question 36. The phenomenon of fertilization was first perceived by – (1) Weismann (2) Leeuwenhoek (3) Robert Hooke (4) Hertwig. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 4, Hertwig. Oscar Hertwig was the first to fully describe the fusion of sperm and egg nuclei in sea urchins. Leeuwenhoek discovered sperm, but Hertwig understood fertilization. Question 37. In reproduction of a test tube baby – (1) Fertilization is done outside body (2) Foetus is grown in a test tube (3) Fertilization is done inside the body (4) None of these. <pause duration='3'/> The answer is 1. In IVF, or 'test tube baby' procedure, fertilization of the egg by the sperm is done in a lab dish, outside the body. The resulting embryo is then transferred to the uterus. Question 38. The final event in the process of fertilization is – (1) Fusion of gametes (2) Egg activation (3) Amphimixis (4) Organizational change in egg cytoplasm. <pause duration='3'/> The answer is 3, Amphimixis. This is the specific term for the fusion of the male and female pronuclei inside the egg, which completes the process of fertilization. Question 39. During fertilization, which centriole of the sperm gives rise to a spindle system inside the egg – (1) Proximal centriole (2) Distal centriole (3) Ring centriole (4) None of the above. <pause duration='3'/> The correct option is 1, Proximal centriole. The sperm contributes its proximal centriole to the egg, which then organizes the mitotic spindle for the first division of the zygote. Question 40. The fertilization membrane during fertilization is synthesized by – (1) Mitochondria (2) Golgi bodies (3) Acid mucopolysaccharides of cortical granules (4) All the above. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 3. After a sperm enters the egg, cortical granules release their contents, including acid mucopolysaccharides, which react to form the tough fertilization membrane. This prevents other sperm from entering. Question 41. The function of egg cell is/are – (1) It supplies a haploid set of chromosomes to the future embryo (2) It provides most of the cytoplasm to the embryo (3) It supplies food reserves to the embryo (4) All the above. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 4, All the above. The egg cell does all of this: it provides the maternal haploid chromosomes, the initial cytoplasm, and the necessary food reserves (yolk) for the early embryo. Question 42. The size of the egg chiefly depends upon the – (1) Size of the animal (2) Amount of yolk reserves in it (3) Amount of food taken by the mother (4) All the above. <pause duration='3'/> The answer is 2, the amount of yolk reserves in it. Eggs of animals like birds, which develop externally, are very large because they need a lot of yolk for nourishment. Mammalian eggs are tiny as the embryo gets nutrition from the mother. Question 43. When more than one sperm establish contact and penetrate into the egg, then it is called – (1) Monospermy (2) Polyspermy (3) (1) and (2) both (4) None of these. <pause duration='3'/> Correct! This is called 2, Polyspermy. It's a condition where an egg is fertilized by more than one sperm, which is usually lethal for the embryo. The fertilization membrane helps prevent this. Question 44. The disadvantage of parthenogenesis is – (1) Establishment of polyploid generation (2) Elimination of variety in population (3) Means of reproduction (4) Does not encourage the appearance of new and advantageous combinations of genes. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 4. While parthenogenesis is a rapid means of reproduction, its main drawback is that it doesn't create genetic variation, which is crucial for a population to adapt to changing environments. Option 2 is also a consequence of this. However, option 4 is the most precise answer. Question 45. Which type of stimulus is used to achieve parthenogenic development – (1) Chemical (2) Change in pH (3) Pricking with microneedle (4) All the above. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 4, All the above. Artificial parthenogenesis can be induced in some species by various physical or chemical stimuli, like changes in pH, temperature, or even pricking the egg with a needle. Question 46. The primordial germ cells make their appearance from wall of – (1) Amnion (2) Chorion (3) Allantois (4) Gonad. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 4, Gonad. Primordial germ cells are the precursors to gametes. While they originate elsewhere early in development, they migrate to and populate the developing gonads (testes or ovaries). Question 47. Megasporangium is equivalent to : (1) Ovule (2) Embryo sac (3) Fruit (4) Nucellus. <pause duration='3'/> The correct answer is 1, Ovule.
