## Mathematics: Calculus (Definite Integrals)

### Topic: The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

The **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus** connects differentiation and integration. It states that if a function \( f \) is continuous on the interval \([a, b]\) and \( F \) is an antiderivative of \( f \), then the definite integral of \( f \) from \( a \) to \( b \) is the difference \( F(b) - F(a) \).

This theorem allows us to calculate the area under a curve without using Riemann sums.

**Formula:**

\[
\int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)
\]

## Geometry: Circles and Cylinders

### Topic: Area of a Sector and Volume

In geometry, dealing with curved shapes requires the constant \( \pi \).  
A **sector of a circle** is a portion of the circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.

If the angle \( \theta \) is in radians and the radius is \( r \), the area of the sector is given by:

\[
A = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta
\]

For three-dimensional objects such as a **cylinder**, the volume is calculated by multiplying the area of the circular base by the height \( h \).

**Formulas:**

- **Area of a Sector:**
  \[
  A = \frac{1}{2} r^2 \theta
  \]

- **Volume of a Cylinder:**
  \[
  V = \pi r^2 h
  \]

## Physics: Wave Mechanics

### Topic: Wave Period and Frequency

Waves transfer energy from one point to another without transferring matter. The relationship between wave speed \( v \), frequency \( f \), and wavelength \( \lambda \) is fundamental to understanding sound and light.

The **period** \( T \) is the time it takes for one complete wave cycle to pass a given point and is the inverse of frequency.

**Formulas:**

\[
v = f \lambda
\]

\[
T = \frac{1}{f}
\]

## Chemistry: Chemical Equilibrium

### Topic: The Equilibrium Constant (\( K_c \))

Reversible reactions do not go to completion. Instead, they reach a state where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction.

For a general reaction:

\[
aA + bB \rightleftharpoons cC + dD
\]

the equilibrium constant \( K_c \) expresses the ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations, each raised to the power of their stoichiometric coefficients.

**Formula:**

\[
K_c = \frac{[C]^c [D]^d}{[A]^a [B]^b}
\]

## Biology: Cell Evolution

### Topic: The Endosymbiotic Theory

The **Endosymbiotic Theory** explains the origin of eukaryotic cells from prokaryotic organisms. It proposes that key organelles—specifically **mitochondria** and **chloroplasts**—were once free-living bacteria that were engulfed by a larger ancestral host cell.

Instead of being digested, these bacteria formed a symbiotic relationship with the host cell:

- Mitochondria provided energy through cellular respiration.
- Chloroplasts provided food through photosynthesis.
- The host cell provided protection and resources.

Over millions of years of evolution, these bacteria lost their independence and became specialized organelles. This theory is supported by evidence such as mitochondria having their own DNA, separate from the cell nucleus.
