# Chemical Reactions and Stoichiometry

## Topic 1: Types of Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions involve the rearrangement of atoms to form new substances. There are five main types:

### Synthesis (Combination) Reactions
Two or more reactants combine to form a single product.
$$A + B \rightarrow AB$$

Example: Formation of water
$$2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$$

### Decomposition Reactions
A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances.
$$AB \rightarrow A + B$$

Example: Electrolysis of water
$$2H_2O \rightarrow 2H_2 + O_2$$

### Single Replacement Reactions
One element replaces another in a compound.
$$A + BC \rightarrow AC + B$$

Example: Zinc in copper sulfate
$$Zn + CuSO_4 \rightarrow ZnSO_4 + Cu$$

### Double Replacement Reactions
Ions from two compounds exchange partners.
$$AB + CD \rightarrow AD + CB$$

Example: Precipitation reaction
$$AgNO_3 + NaCl \rightarrow AgCl \downarrow + NaNO_3$$

### Combustion Reactions
A substance reacts with oxygen, releasing energy as heat and light.

Example: Burning methane
$$CH_4 + 2O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + 2H_2O + energy$$

## Topic 2: Balancing Chemical Equations

Chemical equations must obey the Law of Conservation of Mass - atoms cannot be created or destroyed.

### Steps to Balance Equations

1. Write the unbalanced equation
2. Count atoms of each element on both sides
3. Add coefficients to balance atoms
4. Check that all atoms are balanced
5. Verify coefficients are in lowest whole-number ratio

### Example: Balancing Combustion of Propane

Unbalanced: $C_3H_8 + O_2 \rightarrow CO_2 + H_2O$

Balanced: $C_3H_8 + 5O_2 \rightarrow 3CO_2 + 4H_2O$

Verification:
- Carbon: 3 = 3 ✓
- Hydrogen: 8 = 8 ✓
- Oxygen: 10 = 10 ✓

## Topic 3: Stoichiometry Calculations

Stoichiometry uses mole ratios from balanced equations to calculate quantities in chemical reactions.

### The Mole Concept
- 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number)
- Molar mass = mass in grams of 1 mole of substance

### Mole-to-Mole Calculations

For the reaction: $2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$

The mole ratio tells us:
- 2 moles H₂ react with 1 mole O₂
- 2 moles H₂ produce 2 moles H₂O

### Limiting Reactant
The reactant that runs out first, limiting the amount of product formed.

## Worked Example

How many grams of water are produced when 4.0 g of H₂ reacts with excess O₂?

**Solution:**
1. Moles of H₂ = 4.0 g ÷ 2.0 g/mol = 2.0 mol
2. From equation: 2 mol H₂ → 2 mol H₂O
3. Moles of H₂O = 2.0 mol
4. Mass of H₂O = 2.0 mol × 18.0 g/mol = 36.0 g
