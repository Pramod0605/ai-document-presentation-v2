# Cell Biology: Structure and Function

## Topic 1: The Cell Membrane

The cell membrane, also known as the plasma membrane, is a phospholipid bilayer that surrounds all living cells. It acts as a selective barrier, controlling what enters and exits the cell.

### Structure of the Phospholipid Bilayer

The membrane consists of:
- **Phospholipid molecules** with hydrophilic (water-loving) heads facing outward
- **Hydrophobic (water-fearing) tails** facing inward
- **Cholesterol molecules** embedded to maintain fluidity
- **Integral proteins** that span the entire membrane
- **Peripheral proteins** attached to the surface

This arrangement creates the fluid mosaic model, where proteins float within the lipid bilayer like icebergs in a sea.

### Transport Across the Membrane

Substances cross the membrane through several mechanisms:

1. **Passive Transport** - No energy required
   - Simple diffusion: Small nonpolar molecules
   - Facilitated diffusion: Larger molecules via channel or carrier proteins
   - Osmosis: Water movement through aquaporins

2. **Active Transport** - Requires ATP energy
   - Sodium-potassium pump: Moves 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in
   - Vesicular transport: Endocytosis and exocytosis

## Topic 2: Cellular Organelles

Eukaryotic cells contain membrane-bound organelles, each with specialized functions:

### The Nucleus
- Contains DNA organized into chromosomes
- Nuclear envelope with nuclear pores
- Nucleolus produces ribosomal RNA
- Controls cell activities through gene expression

### Mitochondria - The Powerhouse
- Double membrane structure
- Inner membrane folded into cristae
- Produces ATP through cellular respiration
- Has its own circular DNA (maternal inheritance)
- Performs oxidative phosphorylation

### Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)
- **Rough ER**: Studded with ribosomes, synthesizes proteins
- **Smooth ER**: No ribosomes, synthesizes lipids, detoxifies drugs

### Golgi Apparatus
- Stack of flattened membrane sacs (cisternae)
- Modifies, packages, and sorts proteins
- Produces lysosomes and secretory vesicles

## Topic 3: Cell Division - Mitosis

Mitosis is the process of nuclear division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells.

### Stages of Mitosis

1. **Prophase**: Chromosomes condense, nuclear envelope breaks down
2. **Metaphase**: Chromosomes align at the cell equator
3. **Anaphase**: Sister chromatids separate and move to poles
4. **Telophase**: Nuclear envelopes reform, chromosomes decondense

Cytokinesis follows, dividing the cytoplasm to create two separate cells.
