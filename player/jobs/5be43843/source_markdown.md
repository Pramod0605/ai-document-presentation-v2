## 3.1 Introduction

In mathematics and in real life, many situations involve two unknown quantities that depend on each other simultaneously. For example, when we buy two different items together, calculate ages of two people, or determine distance and speed, we are often dealing with two variables connected by two conditions. To represent such situations mathematically, we use pair of linear equations in two variables.

A linear equation in two variables represents a straight line when drawn on a graph. When two such equations describe the same situation, their common solution gives values of the variables that satisfy both equations at the same time. This makes the chapter extremely important because it helps convert real-life word problems into mathematical equations and then solve them systematically.

This chapter builds a strong foundation for coordinate geometry, algebraic reasoning, and higher mathematics such as matrices, determinants, and linear programming. From an examination point of view, this chapter carries high weightage and includes case-study and HOTS questions regularly.

### Basic Form

A pair of linear equations in two variables is written as:

$$a1x + b1y + c1 = 0$$

$$a2x + b2y + c2 = 0$$

#### Examples (Introduction Concept)

#### Example 1

Two numbers have a sum of 10 and their difference is 2. Find the numbers.

###### Step 1: Let

First number = x

Second number = y

Step 2: Form equations

$$x + y = 10$$

$$x - y = 2$$

This is a pair of linear equations in two variables.

Let:

Cost of one pencil = y

$$4x + 5y = 37$$

Example 3

Let:

Breadth = x

Equation:

$$2(x + x + 4) = 36$$

#### IMPORTANT QUESTIONS – INTRODUCTION (SOLVED)

1. Define a pair of linear equations in two variables.

Answer:

A pair of linear equations in two variables consists of two linear equations involving the same two variables, usually  $x$  and  $y$ . Each equation is of degree one. The solution of the pair is the value of the variables that satisfies both equations at the same time.

Example:

$$2x + 3y = 7$$

$$x - y = 1$$

2. Write the general form of a linear equation in two variables.

Answer:

The general form of a linear equation in two variables is:

$$ax + by + c = 0$$

where  $a$ ,  $b$  and  $c$  are real numbers and  $a$  and  $b$  are not both zero.

3. Why are two equations required to find two unknowns?

Answer:

One linear equation in two variables has infinitely many solutions. It does not give a unique value for the variables. To find unique values of two unknowns, two independent equations are required. The common solution of the two equations gives the required values of the unknowns.

4. Give one real-life example of a pair of linear equations.

Answer:

The cost of 2 pens and 1 notebook is Rs 25 and the cost of 3 pens and 1 notebook is Rs 35.

Let the cost of one pen be  $x$  and the cost of one notebook be  $y$ .

Then the pair of linear equations is:

$$2x + y = 25$$

$$3x + y = 35$$

5. Can a single linear equation in two variables have a unique solution? Explain.

Answer:

No, a single linear equation in two variables cannot have a unique solution. This is because a linear equation in two variables represents a straight line and every point on the line satisfies the equation. Hence, it has infinitely many solutions. A unique solution is possible only when two linear equations are given.

## 3.2 Graphical Method of Solution (Very Detailed Explanation)

In the graphical method, each linear equation is represented as a straight line on the Cartesian plane. The point of intersection of the two lines, if it exists, gives the solution of the pair of equations.

This method provides a visual understanding of solutions and helps identify whether a solution exists, whether a unique solution exists, whether infinitely many solutions exist, or whether no solution exists.

### Types of Graphical Solutions

#### - Intersecting Lines

There is one common point, hence one unique solution. The pair of equations is consistent.

#### - Parallel Lines

There is no common point, hence no solution. The pair of equations is inconsistent.

#### - Coincident Lines

There are infinitely many common points, hence infinitely many solutions. The pair of equations is dependent and consistent.

### Graphical Examples (Step-by-Step)

#### Example 1 (Intersecting Lines)

$$x + y = 5$$

$$2x - y = 1$$

If  $x = 0$ ,  $y = -1$

If  $y = 0$ ,  $x = 1/2$

###### Step 2: Draw both lines on graph paper.

They intersect at (2, 3).

Final Answer:

$$x = 2, y = 3$$

#### Example 2 (Parallel Lines)

$$x + 2y = 4$$

$$2x + 4y = 10$$

The ratios of coefficients of  $x$  and  $y$  are equal, but the ratio of constants is different.

Therefore, the lines are parallel.

Final Answer: No solution.

#### Example 3 (Coincident Lines)

$$2x + 3y = 6$$

$$4x + 6y = 12$$

The second equation is a multiple of the first equation.

Therefore, the lines coincide.

Final Answer: Infinitely many solutions.

## 3.3 Algebraic Methods of Solving a Pair of Linear Equations

The graphical method is not accurate when solutions involve fractions or decimals. Therefore, algebraic methods are preferred.

### 3.3.1 Substitution Method (Detailed Explanation)

In this method, one variable is expressed in terms of the other variable using one equation. This value is then substituted into the second equation. The resulting equation contains only one variable, which can be solved easily.

Examples (Substitution Method)

Example 1

$$x + y = 7$$

$$x - y = 1$$

From the first equation:

$$x = 7 - y$$

Substitute in the second equation:

$$7 - y - y = 1$$

$$2y = 6$$

$$y = 3$$

$$x = 4$$

Final Answer:

$$x = 4, y = 3$$

Example 2

$$2x + y = 11$$

$$x + y = 7$$

Subtract the second equation from the first:

$$x = 4$$

$$y = 3$$

Final Answer:

$$x = 4, y = 3$$

Example 3

$$3x + 2y = 13$$

$$x + y = 5$$

From second equation:

$$y = 5 - x$$

Substitute:

$$3x + 2(5 - x) = 13$$

$$x = 3$$

$$y = 2$$

Final Answer:

$$x = 3, y = 2$$

#### HOTS QUESTIONS – PAIR OF LINEAR EQUATIONS IN TWO VARIABLES (DIFFICULT – STEP BY STEP)

##### QUESTION 1

Can a pair of linear equations have exactly two solutions? Explain.

ANSWER:

No, a pair of linear equations in two variables cannot have exactly two solutions.

###### EXPLANATION:

Each linear equation represents a straight line. Two straight lines in a plane can either:

1. Intersect at one point (one solution)
2. Coincide completely (infinitely many solutions)
3. Be parallel and never intersect (no solution)

There is no possible position of two straight lines that gives exactly two points of intersection.

###### CONCLUSION:

A pair of linear equations cannot have exactly two solutions.

##### QUESTION 2

If both equations represent the same line, why are infinite solutions possible?

###### ANSWER:

If both equations represent the same straight line, then every point on that line satisfies both equations.

###### EXPLANATION:

- Coinciding lines overlap completely.
- A straight line contains infinitely many points.
- Each point gives a different solution  $(x, y)$ .

Hence, there are infinitely many solutions.

###### CONCLUSION:

When both equations represent the same line, the pair has infinitely many solutions.

##### QUESTION 3

If coefficients of  $x$  and  $y$  are proportional but constants are different, explain graphically.

###### ANSWER:

Consider the pair of equations:

$$a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$$

$$a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$$

Given:

$$a_1 / a_2 = b_1 / b_2 \neq c_1 / c_2$$

GRAPHICAL INTERPRETATION:

- Coefficients of  $x$  and  $y$  being proportional means both lines have the same slope.
- Different constants mean the lines have different intercepts.
- Therefore, the lines are parallel.

RESULT:

Parallel lines never intersect.

CONCLUSION:

The given pair of linear equations has no solution.

QUESTION 4

Find the value of  $k$  for which the following pair of linear equations has no solution:

$$kx + y = 3$$

$$6x + 2y = 8$$

STEP 1: Write equations in standard form

$$kx + y - 3 = 0$$

$$6x + 2y - 8 = 0$$

STEP 2: Compare coefficients

$$a_1 = k, b_1 = 1, c_1 = -3$$

$$a_2 = 6, b_2 = 2, c_2 = -8$$

STEP 3: Condition for no solution

$$a_1 / a_2 = b_1 / b_2 \neq c_1 / c_2$$

STEP 4: Apply condition

$$k / 6 = 1 / 2$$

$$k = 3$$

Check constants:

$$-3 / -8 \neq 1 / 2$$

CONCLUSION:

For  $k = 3$ , the given pair of linear equations has no solution.

##### QUESTION 5

Solve a real-life word problem involving ratios and savings.

The ratio of savings of A and B is  $3 : 5$ . After A saves Rs 400 more and B saves Rs 200 more, their savings become equal. Find their original savings.

$$\text{Original savings of B} = \text{Rs } 500$$

#### HOTS QUESTION 1

Find the value of  $k$  for which the following pair of linear equations has

(i) no solution  
(ii) infinitely many solutions

$$kx + 3y = 5$$

$$6x + ky = 10$$

Solution:

$$\text{Equation (1): } kx + 3y - 5 = 0$$

$$\text{Equation (2): } 6x + ky - 10 = 0$$

Comparing coefficients:

$$a_1 = k, b_1 = 3, c_1 = -5$$

$$a_2 = 6, b_2 = k, c_2 = -10$$

For no solution:

$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$

$$\frac{k}{6} = \frac{3}{k} \neq \frac{5}{10}$$

$$\text{From } \frac{k}{6} = \frac{3}{k}$$

$$k^2 = 18$$

$$k = \pm\sqrt{18}$$

$$\text{Check } \frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{1}{2}, \text{ which is not equal to } \frac{\sqrt{18}}{6}$$

Hence, for  $k = \pm\sqrt{18}$ , the pair has **no solution**.

For infinitely many solutions:

$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$

$$\frac{k}{6} = \frac{3}{k} = \frac{5}{10}$$

$$\text{From } \frac{3}{k} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$k = 6$$

Final Answer:

No solution when  $k = \pm\sqrt{18}$

Infinitely many solutions when  $k = 6$

#### HOTS QUESTION 2

If the system of equations

$$2x + 3y = 11$$

$$4x + ky = 22$$

has infinitely many solutions, find the value of  $k$ .

Solution:

For infinitely many solutions:

$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$

$$\frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{k} = \frac{11}{22}$$

$$\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\frac{11}{22} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\text{So, } \frac{3}{k} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$k = 6$$

Final Answer:

$$k = 6$$

#### HOTS QUESTION 3

For what value of  $k$  does the following pair of equations represent parallel lines?

$$3x + ky = 7$$

$$6x + 4y = 14$$

Solution:

Parallel lines condition:

$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$

$$\frac{3}{6} = \frac{k}{4} \neq \frac{7}{14}$$

$$\frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$\frac{7}{14} = \frac{1}{2}$$

So, to make lines parallel:

$$\frac{k}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$

$$k = 2$$

But  $\frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{1}{2}$ , which makes equations coincident.

To make them parallel,  $k$  must be different.

Hence, **no value of  $k$**  makes them parallel.

Final Answer:

There is no value of  $k$  for which the lines are parallel.

#### HOTS QUESTION 4

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 10. The number formed by reversing the digits is 36 more than the original number. Find the number.

Solution:

Let the tens digit =  $x$

Let the units digit =  $y$

Original number =  $10x + y$

Reversed number =  $10y + x$

According to the question:

$$x + y = 10 \dots (1)$$

$$10y + x = 10x + y + 36$$

Simplifying equation (2):

$$9y - 9x = 36$$

$$y - x = 4 \dots(2)$$

Solving (1) and (2):

Add both equations:

$$2y = 14$$

$$y = 7$$

Substitute in (1):

$$x + 7 = 10$$

$$x = 3$$

Final Answer:

The number is 37

#### HOTS QUESTION 5

The difference between the incomes of two persons is ₹2000. The difference between their expenditures is ₹1500. If each saves ₹500, find their incomes.

Solution:

Let income of first person = x

Let income of second person = y

Given:

$$x - y = 2000 \dots(1)$$

Savings = Income - Expenditure

Expenditures:

$$x - 500 \text{ and } y - 500$$

Difference of expenditures:

$$(x - 500) - (y - 500) = 1500$$

$$x - y = 1500 \dots(2)$$

From (1) and (2):

$$2000 \neq 1500$$

This gives a contradiction.

Final Answer:

The given data is inconsistent. No solution exists.

#### HOTS QUESTION 6

Find the value of  $m$  for which the pair of equations has a unique solution.

$$mx + 2y = 6$$

$$3x + my = 9$$

Solution:

Unique solution condition:

$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$$

$$\frac{m}{3} \neq \frac{2}{m}$$

$$m^2 \neq 6$$

$$m \neq \pm\sqrt{6}$$

Final Answer:

The system has a unique solution for all values of  $m$  except  $\pm\sqrt{6}$ .

#### HOTS QUESTION 7

Two straight lines intersect at a point. Can the system of equations have more than one solution? Justify mathematically.

Solution:

If two lines intersect at one point, they have exactly one common point.

Therefore, the system has exactly one solution.

Mathematically:

$$\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$$

Final Answer:

No, intersecting lines always have exactly one solution.

#### HOTS QUESTION 8

The sum of two numbers is 27 and their difference is 3. Find the numbers using elimination method.

Solution:

Let numbers be x and y

$$x + y = 27 \dots(1)$$

$$x - y = 3 \dots(2)$$

Add (1) and (2):

$$2x = 30$$

$$x = 15$$

Substitute in (1):

$$15 + y = 27$$

$$y = 12$$

Final Answer:

The numbers are 15 and 12.

#### HOTS QUESTION 9

If the pair of equations

$$ax + by = c$$

$$bx + ay = c$$

has infinitely many solutions, find the relation between a and b.

Solution:

For infinitely many solutions:

$$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{b}{a} = \frac{c}{c}$$

$$\text{From } \frac{a}{b} = \frac{b}{a}$$

$$a^2 = b^2$$

$$a = b$$

Final Answer:

$$a = b$$

#### HOTS QUESTION 10

The perimeter of a rectangle is 60 cm. Its length is 6 cm more than its breadth. Find its dimensions.

Solution:

$$\text{Let breadth} = x$$

$$\text{Length} = x + 6$$

Perimeter formula:

$$2(l + b) = 60$$

$$2(x + x + 6) = 60$$

$$2x + 6 = 30$$

$$2x = 24$$

$$x = 12$$

$$\text{Length} = 18$$

Final Answer:

$$\text{Length} = 18 \text{ cm}$$

$$\text{Breadth} = 12 \text{ cm}$$

### GRAPH-BASED HOTS QUESTIONS

#### GRAPH-BASED HOTS QUESTION 1

Draw the graphs of the following pair of linear equations and determine the nature of their solution.

$$x - y = 1$$

$$2x - 2y = 2$$

Solution:

Step 1: Write both equations in slope-intercept form.

From equation (1):

$$x - y = 1$$

$$y = x - 1$$

From equation (2):

$$2x - 2y = 2$$

Divide by 2:

$$x - y = 1$$

$$y = x - 1$$

Step 2: Observe that both equations represent the same line.

Step 3: Draw the graph.

Graph drawing steps:

For  $y = x - 1$

Take points:

$$x = 0, y = -1$$

$$x = 1, y = 0$$

Plot these points and draw a straight line.

Step 4: Since both equations give the same line, the graphs coincide.

Final Answer:

The pair of linear equations has infinitely many solutions.

#### GRAPH-BASED HOTS QUESTION 2

Solve graphically and hence determine whether the system has a unique solution.

$$x + y = 4$$

$$x - y = 2$$

Solution:

Step 1: Convert equations into graph-friendly form.

Equation (1):

$$x + y = 4$$

$$y = 4 - x$$

Equation (2):

$$x - y = 2$$

$$y = x - 2$$

Step 2: Find points for each line.

For  $y = 4 - x$ :

$$x = 0, y = 4$$

$$x = 4, y = 0$$

For  $y = x - 2$ :

$$x = 0, y = -2$$

$$x = 2, y = 0$$

Step 3: Draw the graph.

Graph drawing steps:

Plot the points for both equations on the same graph paper and draw straight lines.

Step 4: Observe the point of intersection.

The two lines intersect at (3, 1).

Final Answer:

The solution is  $x = 3, y = 1$ .

The pair has a unique solution.

#### GRAPH-BASED HOTS QUESTION 3

By drawing graphs, show that the following pair of equations has no solution.

$$2x + y = 5$$

$$4x + 2y = 12$$

Solution:

Step 1: Convert equations into slope-intercept form.

Equation (1):

$$2x + y = 5$$

$$y = 5 - 2x$$

Equation (2):

$$4x + 2y = 12$$

Divide by 2:

$$2x + y = 6$$

$$y = 6 - 2x$$

Step 2: Find points.

For  $y = 5 - 2x$ :

$$x = 0, y = 5$$

$$x = 2, y = 1$$

For  $y = 6 - 2x$ :

$$x = 0, y = 6$$

$$x = 2, y = 2$$

Step 3: Draw the graph.

Graph drawing steps:

Plot both sets of points and draw the lines.

Step 4: Observe the graph.

The lines have the same slope but different intercepts, so they are parallel.

Final Answer:

The pair of equations has no solution.

#### GRAPH-BASED HOTS QUESTION 4

The cost of 1 pen and 2 pencils is ₹9. The cost of 2 pens and 4 pencils is ₹18. Represent the situation graphically and interpret the result.

Solution:

Let:

Cost of one pen =  $x$

Cost of one pencil =  $y$

Equations:

$$x + 2y = 9$$

$$2x + 4y = 18$$

Step 1: Simplify the second equation.

$$2x + 4y = 18$$

Divide by 2:

$$x + 2y = 9$$

Step 2: Both equations are identical.

Step 3: Draw the graph.

Graph drawing steps:

For  $x + 2y = 9$

Take points:

$$x = 1, y = 4$$

$$x = 5, y = 2$$

Plot the points and draw a straight line.

Step 4: Since both equations represent the same line, the graphs coincide.

Final Answer:

The system has infinitely many solutions.

The cost of pen and pencil cannot be uniquely determined.

#### GRAPH-BASED HOTS QUESTION 5

Draw the graphs of the equations

$$x = 2$$

$$y = 3$$

and find the coordinates of the point of intersection. Also state the nature of the solution.

Solution:

Step 1: Understand the equations.

$x = 2$  represents a vertical line parallel to the y-axis.

$y = 3$  represents a horizontal line parallel to the x-axis.

Step 2: Draw the graph.

Graph drawing steps:

Draw a vertical line passing through  $x = 2$ .

Draw a horizontal line passing through  $y = 3$ .

Step 3: Observe the point of intersection.

The two lines intersect at the point  $(2, 3)$ .

Final Answer:

The solution is  $x = 2$ ,  $y = 3$ .

The pair of equations has a unique solution.

Algebraic methods such as substitution and elimination give exact solutions. The nature of the solution depends on the ratios of coefficients. This chapter is very important for board examinations.
